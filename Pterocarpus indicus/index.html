Pterocarpus indicus




Tree Species: Pterocarpus indicus (Fabaceae)
Common Names: Narra, Burmese Rosewood

Audio Script

"Hello! I’m Pterocarpus indicus, commonly known as Narra or Burmese Rosewood. You can recognize me by my wide, shady canopy, sweet-scented yellow flowers, and unique flat, disc-shaped seed pods with papery wings. Like other legumes, my roots host beneficial bacteria that fix nitrogen to enrich campus soil. Historically, my rose-scented red heartwood has been prized for fine furniture and musical instruments, while my leaves and bark have been used in traditional remedies."

Fun Facts

  • Identification: Large semi-deciduous tree with pinnately compound leaves (5–11 ovate leaflets), fragrant yellow flowers in panicles, and distinctive flat, round, winged seed pods
  • Ecological Role: Belongs to the legume (Fabaceae) family; forms symbiotic root nodules with nitrogen-fixing bacteria to improve soil fertility and provides a rich nectar source for honeybees.
  • Medicinal & Economic Uses: Yields valuable, termite-resistant red timber used in fine cabinetry and musical instruments, and serves as the national tree of the Philippines. Leaf and bark infusions are documented in ethnomedicine for soothing skin sores and inflammation.
