African Tulip Tree




Tree Species: Spathodea campanulata (Bignoniaceae)
Common Names: African Tulip Tree, Pichkari, Fountain Tree

Audio Script

"Hello! I’m the African Tulip Tree, scientifically known as Spathodea campanulata. Native to tropical Africa, you can spot me by my glossy compound leaves and striking, cup-shaped orange-red flowers that bloom like flames. My unopened flower buds actually hold water—earning me the local nickname Pichkari! While my cup-shaped blooms provide nectar and rainwater for sunbirds, I’m an introduced species here, so ecologists monitor me carefully to ensure I don’t crowd out native campus plants."

Fun Facts

  • Identification: Medium-to-large evergreen tree with opposite pinnate leaves (7–17 leaflets), upright boat-shaped seed pods, and bright scarlet-orange, bell-shaped flowers with ruffled yellow edges.
  • Ecological Role: Native to tropical West and Central Africa; its cup-shaped flowers collect rainwater and nectar that attract birds and bats. Outside its native range, it is tracked as a fast-growing introduced ornamental that can outcompete native flora.
  • Medicinal & Economic Uses: Nicknamed the Pichkari or Fountain Tree because its closed flower buds squirt water when squeezed. In traditional African medicine, bark and leaf preparations are used as antiseptic washes, while its soft, fire-resistant wood is used to craft drums.
